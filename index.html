The story of Almond Mosaic

Discover

Origin & storyA tile from the French countryside

Almond Mosaic is inspired by tuiles aux amandes, the almond tile cookies of traditional French bakeries.

Why “tuile”

Tuile means tile. The cookies were named after the curved terracotta roof tiles of country homes, because they were bent over curved rolling pins while still warm from the oven.

Why “mosaic”

Paper-thin almond slices are scattered by hand across a light, golden wafer. Once baked, the pattern looks like fine mosaic work, and it breaks with an ultra-crisp, feather-light snap.

Key ingredientsSix things, nothing more

i.

Sliced almonds

High-grade, finely sliced, for aroma and a delicate crunch.

ii.

Pure egg whites

The base of a true tuile: crisp, airy, never greasy.

iii.

Pure butter

Churned butter for a rich, golden aroma.

iv.

Cane sugar

Unrefined sweetness that never overpowers.

v.

Light wheat flour

Only enough to bind, so it stays crisp.

vi.

Vanilla & sea salt

To lift the butter and deepen the roast.
